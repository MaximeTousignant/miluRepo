\section{Foreword}
% ----------------------------------------------------------------------

An economic system is a set of accounting rules governing the mechanisms by which a
currency is created, destroyed and exchanged. A currency is a quantity of liquid money.
Liquid money feeds a mechanism of social hierarchy. Money is what makes other umans do
what we want them to do with their uman$\cdot$time: the paid week.

The conventional economic system dates from the age of paper. It was designed so that
people could write its transactions by hand, with a pencil, on paper, in ledgers. It was
designed so that a uman could carry out transfer operations mentally --- adding and
subtracting money from one account to another by the sheer computing power of their
brain. The uman brain is good at many things, but it is hopeless at arithmetic.

We no longer live in the age of paper. We live in the age of the Internet. Each of us
carries a computer able to compute additions and multiplications millions of times faster
than a uman, and without ever making a mistake. These pocket computers all communicate
with one another, exchanging information at the speed of light anywhere on Earth, without
interruption and without error.

This new reality has opened doors in the field of economic cooperation models that have
so far been explored only timidly. The t\^{o}k system is one of those explorations, and
its starting point is the principle of a universal basic income.

\section{External data}

\subsection{Universal time}

The t\^{o}k system assumes that there exists a universal time, flowing at the same rate
for all umans. This amounts to the approximation of classical, non-relativistic physics.
As long as we have no means of transport whose speed approaches that of light, this
approximation will remain entirely valid.

In practice, the t\^{o}k system uses
\href{https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coordinated_Universal_Time}{Coordinated Universal
Time} (UTC). In \texttt{tokRepo} we specifically use the Python implementation of the
\texttt{datetime} library.

\subsection{The uman population}

We assume that sufficiently reliable data exist on the current uman population and on its
history. In practice, we use World Bank data \cite{WorldBankPopulation} from 1960 onward,
and Our World in Data \cite{OwidPopulation} for historical data before 1960.

\section{The conservation law of the t\^{o}k system}
% ----------------------------------------------------------------------

The fundamental principle of the t\^{o}k system is the universal basic income. That
principle is stated rigorously in uman language in the definition of the t\^{o}k. Stated
mathematically, the universal basic income gives rise to a fundamental conservation law
of uman$\cdot$time in the t\^{o}k system.

\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.5pt} % ------------------------------------
\subsection*{Definition of the t\^{o}k}

\textbf{1. The unit of currency}\\
The t\^{o}k is the unit of currency of the t\^{o}k system. One t\^{o}k is the quantity of
liquid money that a person receives from their universal basic income in 15 days.

\textbf{2. The universal basic income}\\
Every person continuously receives the universal basic income of 1 t\^{o}k per 15 days,
between their birth and their death. The universal basic income is the one and only
mechanism by which liquid money is created in the t\^{o}k system.

\textbf{3. Decay}\\
T\^{o}ks decay continuously, with a half-life of 50 years. Decay is the one and only
mechanism by which liquid money is destroyed in the t\^{o}k system.

\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.5pt} % ------------------------------------

Statement 1 defines the unit of the t\^{o}k with respect to the universal basic income,
\begin{equation}
    1\,\tok = 1\,\mathrm{Q}\,\dot{\Lambda}\,.
\end{equation}

Statement 2 of the definition of the t\^{o}k stipulates that for every natural person (a
user of type $PP$) there is exactly \emph{one} t\^{o}k cont of type $\cont_{PP}$ holding a
source of t\^{o}ks whose rate is the universal basic income $\dot{\Lambda}$. Consequently,
the total creation of t\^{o}ks is $N_{PP} \, \dot{\Lambda}$.

Statement 3 imposes a global destruction flow equal to $k_D \, a_\Omega$.

Thus the total quantity of t\^{o}ks $a_\Omega$ obeys, at all times, the conservation law
\begin{equation}
    \frac{d a_\Omega}{dt} = N_{PP} \, \dot{\Lambda} - k_D \,a_{\Omega}\,,
    \label{eqConservation}
\end{equation}
which depends only on the world population $N_{PP}$.

Whatever version of the t\^{o}k system is under consideration, this conservation law must
always be respected. Otherwise, it is no longer

\section{The vanilla t\^{o}k system}
% ----------------------------------------------------------------------

The vanilla version of the t\^{o}k system contains only natural persons, who are users of
the t\^{o}k system throughout their lives. Each user holds a single t\^{o}k cont. The
world population is taken to be constant over one year.

Users may make transfers or flows between one another. There are, however, no committees,
no tax, and no foreign currency.

Main cont of a natural person:
\begin{align*}
    \cont_{PP} :& \quad \frac{d a_i}{dt} = \dot{\Lambda} + \sum_j \dot{F}_{ji} - k_D \, a_i \\
    \cont_{\Omega} :& \quad \frac{d a_\Omega}{dt} = N_{PP}\,\dot{\Lambda} - k_D \, a_\Omega
\end{align*}

\begin{equation*}
    \sum_i a_i = a_\Omega
\end{equation*}

\begin{equation*}
    \sum_i \sum_j \dot{F}_{ji} = 0
\end{equation*}

\subsection{Taxation of t\^{o}ks}

The board of directors continuously collects a tax corresponding to a fraction of the
number of t\^{o}ks held by each member of Smoothop (e.g.\ $x$\,\%/year). The taxation rate
is the same for every member and is determined democratically by them. Members may only
vote for a taxation rate strictly greater than 0\,\%/year. The single taxation rate is the
median of the votes.

Main cont of a natural person:
\begin{equation}
    \frac{d a_i}{dt} = \dot{\Lambda} -\left(k_D+k_T(x)\right) \, a_i
\end{equation}

Generic cont:
\begin{equation}
    \frac{d a_i}{dt} = - \left(k_D+k_T(x)\right) \, a_i
\end{equation}

The board of directors $\overline{\Omega}$ is elected by the general assembly $\Omega^*$,
which comprises all the members of Smoothop. Smoothop consists of the set of its members
together with its board of directors, $\Omega=\overline{\Omega}\cup\Omega^*$.

\begin{equation}
    \frac{d a_{\overline{\Omega}}}{dt} = k_T(x) \sum_i^{N_{\Omega}} a_i -  \left(k_D+k_T(x)\right) a_{\overline{\Omega}}
\end{equation}

The axis along which the members vote:
\begin{equation}
     k_T(x) = k_{T,0} \,1.01^{\,x}
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}
    k_{T,0} = \frac{-\ln(0.99)}{365.25 \text{ days}} \approx \frac{1}{36\,342\text{ days}}
\end{equation}
